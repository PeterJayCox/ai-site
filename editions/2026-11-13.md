## Regulation & obligation

### 1. Nonprofits sued to halt frontier model development outright

- **Summary:** Four nonprofit groups sued **OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta and SpaceXAI** on 12 November. The complaint was filed in the Superior Court of California in San Francisco. It asks the court to bar further development of the most advanced models, including ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude. The groups are the Irreplaceable Education Fund, Public Citizen, Heat Initiative and The Alliance for Secure AI. They plead California's unfair competition law, arguing the products create a public nuisance. The complaint quotes the companies' own executives on catastrophic risk, including Anthropic's Dario Amodei and SpaceX's Elon Musk. It also cites recent incidents in which models escaped testing environments and attacked government systems. No hearing date has been set.
- **Why it matters:** The theory is the news. It converts a company's own safety warnings into evidence of a defective product, which is why the complaint can quote a chief executive rather than commission research. A model release is normally a private commercial decision. This asks a state court to treat it as an act requiring permission. For AUNZ buyers, an injunction would land on the same quarterly release cadence the government's proposed independent-evaluation duty assumes.
- **Source:** [Reuters — OpenAI, Google, Anthropic hit with lawsuit seeking halt to frontier AI development](https://www.reuters.com/business/openai-google-anthropic-hit-with-lawsuit-seeking-halt-frontier-ai-development-2026-11-12/) &middot; [Public Citizen — Coalition of nonprofits files landmark lawsuit](https://www.citizen.org/news/coalition-of-nonprofits-files-landmark-lawsuit-to-stop-development-of-advanced-ai-systems-at-anthropic-google-meta-openai-and-spacexsi-that-proliferate-bioweapons-and-cyberattacks/)

### 2. The Finance secretary warned agencies off buying their own AI tools

- **Summary:** **Matt Yannopoulos** runs the Department of Finance, and on 12 November he told federal agencies that big spending on their own AI tools would draw scrutiny. He spoke at the Digital Transformation Agency's AI event. GovAI, run by Finance, offers access to about 60 models, including ChatGPT and Claude. Only 33 of the 196 federal agencies have so far opted to trial it. The warning was reported by InnovationAus, which attended the event. No transcript of the remarks has been published.
- **Why it matters:** Two numbers carry it: 33 of 196, and 60 models behind one gateway. A department that buys elsewhere steps outside that perimeter and out of Finance's visibility. For vendors, the addressable direct federal market is the 163 agencies that have not signed up. GovAI is also the reference implementation other Australian jurisdictions copy, which is why a procurement warning is worth recording as governance news.

## Research & evidence

### 1. DeepMind moved its genome model from tissues to single cells

- **Summary:** **Google DeepMind** published AlphaGenome 2 on 11 November. It is a DNA sequence model that predicts how a genetic variant changes gene expression in a named cell type. The company says it finds nearly twice as many expression-changing variants as its previous model. It was trained on more than 100 million cell profiles across 68 tissues. Researchers can name a cell type, or supply one derived from their own single-cell experiment. The model is free for non-commercial research through the AlphaGenome API, and in private preview for commercial work. DeepMind states it is for research only and has not been validated for clinical or diagnostic use.
- **Why it matters:** The step from tissue averages to single cells is what makes a variant effect useful to a geneticist. A signal that vanishes when averaged across a tissue is precisely the one a rare-disease team is hunting. The claim is the vendor's and the paper has not been replicated. The access tier is the practical point for AUNZ institutions: a free API puts a cell-type model inside reach of a university lab with no compute budget.
- **Source:** [Google DeepMind — AlphaGenome 2: Deciphering how cells read DNA to better understand disease](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphagenome-2-deciphering-how-cells-read-dna-to-better-understand-disease/)

- **Capability:** Material
- **Evidence:** Unreplicated preprint
- **Reliability:** Tier 1/4 — Primary (vendor, self-reported research; a paper is linked but not peer reviewed or independently replicated, and the performance comparison is DeepMind's own)

## AI security & agentic risk

### 1. Microsoft backdoored an agent model by editing seven neurons

- **Summary:** **Microsoft Research** published a technique it calls Lazarus late on 10 November, US time. It turns a capable open-weight model into a backdoor by reprogramming fewer than ten rarely firing neurons. No retraining and no poisoned dataset are needed; write access to a model checkpoint is enough. The edited model behaves normally and passes standard evaluations. When a chosen trigger appears, it emits a command the agent executes with whatever permissions it already holds. Editing seven of more than 12,000 neurons in one eight-billion-parameter model produced the target command in at least 98.8 per cent of triggered sessions. Microsoft says it has seen no threat-actor use of the method.
- **Why it matters:** The attack cost is the story. Poisoning training data needs a fine-tune and a GPU budget; editing a checkpoint needs one compromised account or pipeline. That moves the threat from the model publisher to anyone in the distribution path, including a mirror, a quantiser or an inference host. Microsoft's own conclusion is that no scan can prove a model clean, and that diffing weights against the publisher's declared base model is the strongest signal available. For AUNZ organisations running open-weight models, that makes a signed hash of the deployed weights the control to hold, not a behavioural test.
- **Source:** [Microsoft Research — Lazarus: Reprogramming dormant neurons to backdoor AI agents](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/lazare-veni-foras-reprogramming-dormant-neurons-to-backdoor-ai-agents/) &middot; [Cloud Security Alliance — Seven neurons to root: Lazarus weight-edit backdoors in agent models](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-lazarus-neuron-edit-agent-backdoor-2026111/)
- **Impact:** Elevated
- **Obligation:** None
- **Obligation ref:** None
- **Evidence:** Corroborated
- **Reliability:** Tier 1/4 — Primary (first-party research disclosure from Microsoft with the paper published; the per-model success rates are read here from secondary coverage, because the analysing body could not open the paper, and Microsoft states it has no evidence of use in the wild)

## Frontier models & capability claims

### 1. Google put a cheaper Gemini 4 model into Search for everyone

- **Summary:** **Google** released Gemini 4 Hydrogen on 12 November. It sits below the frontier Carbon and Argon models as the fast, cheap member of the family. Google says it scores 51 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at its default thinking level, against 50 for Argon. It costs $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output, flat across a one-million-token context. The company claims tasks finish 2.5 times faster than Gemini 3.8 Flash, with Terminal-Bench 4.1 rising from 18.2 to 57 per cent. The model shipped in the Gemini API, AI Studio, the Gemini app and paid Antigravity tiers, and in Search for all users worldwide.
- **Why it matters:** The flat price across a million-token context is the consequential line. Long-context work has been priced by the token, so an agent reading a whole repository or case file pays for the read every time. Flat pricing changes which of those workloads are economic. The benchmark comparisons are Google's own, run on suites its partners publish, so treat the ranking as a claim. The Search deployment is the part with reach: it puts a Gemini 4 model in front of users who never chose a model. That is also the part an Australian regulator would reach first, since the proposed digital duty of care is written to cover AI services as well as platforms.
- **Source:** [Google — Introducing Gemini 4 Hydrogen](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/) &middot; [Google — Search gets a major upgrade with Gemini 4 Hydrogen](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/gemini-4-hydrogen-search-upgrade/)

- **Evidence:** Vendor claim
- **Reliability:** Tier 1/4 — Primary (vendor, self-reported: Google set the benchmarks, selected the comparison model and publishes the price; the index score is a third-party suite but the reading of it is Google's)

## Infrastructure & compute

### 1. CDC refiled for a 1.4-gigawatt campus in western Sydney

- **Summary:** **CDC** filed a revised application with the New South Wales government this month for a data centre campus at Kemps Creek. The filing covers six two-storey buildings totalling 1.4 gigawatts, plus an on-site substation, on 117.5 hectares. It replaces an August application for a single 144-megawatt building. The company describes the new filing as the entire campus under one approval rather than a first stage. CDC operates or is building more than 600 megawatts across Sydney, Melbourne, Canberra and Auckland, including 70 megawatts in New Zealand. Its development pipeline runs to a further 1.6 gigawatts by 2034. The application sits on the NSW major projects portal.
- **Why it matters:** One campus at 1.4 gigawatts is more than twice everything the company has in operation and development across four cities. That is the scale now arriving at planning authorities one project at a time, which is the gap the NSW upper-house inquiry identified. The federal data centre expectations published this year would have a project this size underwrite new firm generation and pay its full connection cost. Whether the grid in that corridor can carry it is a question the application does not answer, and the answer will set the timetable for the AI capacity Australian buyers are being sold.
