# Executive Summary

**Top stories.** Washington moved from policy to instrument. The **Department of Homeland Security** order excluding **DeepSeek** was published in the **Federal Register** on **20 October**, giving the US its first binding *procurement* exclusion against a named AI company under the **Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Security Act** rather than an internal usage policy: it names **Hangzhou DeepSeek Artificial Intelligence Co., Ltd.** and its affiliates, reaches any third-party product or service *developed using* DeepSeek's models, exempts the **Department of Defense**, and allows agencies to seek exceptions in writing within **60 days**. Second, a **37-page** US federal discussion draft, marked privileged and confidential with a **29 September 2026** last-edit date, circulated publicly on **19 October**; on its reported text it would make the **Department of Commerce** the regulator of the most powerful models, require outside evaluators to grade them every **180 days**, report dangerous incidents within **15 days**, and let a single federal court in Washington **suspend a model for an initial 45 days** — while **pre-empting** state safety-disclosure, incident-reporting and evaluation rules. It has **not been introduced in Congress** and the document never names an author.

Two things follow. First, **the AI-governance argument has shifted from whether to regulate to who holds the pen**: a leaked draft that would concentrate frontier-model rules in one federal court sits opposite the EU's product-level regime, the UK's CMA-style intervention and Australia's systems-based approach, and the draft's provenance is asserted rather than evidenced, so its value this week is as a signal of intent, not a legislative fact. Second, **the instrument that did land is a procurement one** — FASCSA excludes a vendor rather than governing a model, which is the pattern AUNZ agencies already have available and have not used; Australia has no equivalent exclusion in force. Third, the money: the **Wall Street Journal**, citing a **draft IPO prospectus**, reported that **Anthropic** recorded a net loss of more than **US$80 billion** in the first half of 2026 — including **US$72 billion** of accounting charges on convertible instruments — against **US$16.8 billion** of Q3 revenue and planned **US$518 billion** of cloud and compute obligations.

## Regulation & obligation

### 1. Washington used its supply-chain statute to remove DeepSeek from every US federal civilian agency

- **Summary:** The **Department of Homeland Security** published an order in the **Federal Register** on **20 October** requiring US federal **civilian** agencies to remove **DeepSeek** products — and any third-party product or service developed using DeepSeek's models — from their IT systems, and barring them from procuring DeepSeek technology. The order names the legal entity **Hangzhou DeepSeek Artificial Intelligence Co., Ltd.** and its affiliates, exempts the **Department of Defense**, invokes the **Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Security Act of 2018 (FASCSA)** and follows a recommendation of the **Federal Acquisition Security Council**. DHS says DeepSeek's models present "multiple threat vectors" and that use "may result in the transfer of sensitive data" to China; agencies may seek exceptions in writing within **60 days** of issuance. Beijing's foreign ministry spokesperson **Mao Ning** said China opposes "overstretching the concept of national security and politicizing technology and trade issues". It landed two days after DeepSeek shipped **V4.1-Flash-1017** at unchanged price. **Why it matters:** this is the first binding *procurement* exclusion against a named AI company under FASCSA rather than an internal policy, and its coverage of third-party products built on DeepSeek closes the loophole contractors have been using. For AUNZ buyers it reframes adoption of Chinese open weights as a supply-chain question rather than a price question — and Australian agencies have no equivalent exclusion instrument in force.
- **Source:** [Federal Register — Notice of order regarding exclusion and removal of DeepSeek products from federal civilian executive branch IT systems and procurements](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/20/2026-21447/notice-of-order-regarding-exclusion-and-removal-of-deepseek-products-from-federal-civilian-executive)

### 2. A leaked 37-page US draft would put frontier-model regulation in one federal court — and pre-empt the states

- **Summary:** A **37-page** federal discussion draft, marked "Privileged &amp; Confidential / Attorney Work Product" with a **29 September 2026** last-edit date, reached a wider audience on **19 October** through a LinkedIn post that attributed it to **Anthropic**, citing a **Politico Pro** report by **Owen Dahlkamp**. On the reported text it would make the **Department of Commerce** the regulator of the most powerful models, require outside evaluators to grade them every **180 days**, require safety frameworks and **15-day** incident reporting, and give a single federal court in Washington power to **suspend a model for an initial 45 days**; it also **pre-empts** state safety-disclosure, incident-reporting and evaluation rules while leaving states their laws on AI use, minors and consumer protection. It sets a reported **10^25** compute line and a **US$500 million** revenue test. The short title is offered three ways (including the "**AEGIS Act**"), findings and enforcement sections are empty, and penalties and the order length remain bracketed. It has **not been introduced in Congress**. **Why it matters:** if the attribution holds, this is frontier labs asking for one federal regulator rather than fifty state ones — the opposite posture to product-level intervention in the UK and to Australia's systems-regulation approach — and the pre-emption clause, not the court order, is the load-bearing provision. Treat the attribution and the bracketed terms as **unconfirmed**: the word Anthropic appears nowhere in the document, and the link rests on a secondhand account of a report PPC Land has not itself seen.
- **Source:** [PPC Land — Draft bill tied to Anthropic would let courts halt AI models for 45 days](https://ppc.land/draft-bill-tied-to-anthropic-would-let-courts-halt-ai-models-for-45-days)
- **Impact:** Guarded
- **Obligation:** Proposed
- **Obligation ref:** No instrument in force — unnumbered US federal discussion draft, last edited 29 September 2026, not introduced in Congress; would operate alongside and pre-empt state AI statutes if enacted
- **Evidence:** Rumoured
- **Reliability:** Tier 3/4 — Moderate (a specialist policy outlet's forensic account of a leaked PDF; the corporate attribution rests on secondary reporting and a third-party social post, and the outlet has not seen the underlying report)
