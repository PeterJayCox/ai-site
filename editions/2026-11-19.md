## Regulation & obligation

### 1. California proposed the power to shut a frontier model down

- **Summary:** A report commissioned by **Gavin Newsom**'s September executive order, published on 18 November, recommends that California set baseline safety standards for every AI developer instead of leaving them to companies. It proposes independent safety testing through training, development and deployment, and wider incident reporting. It would give the state authority to order an emergency shutdown, and power to cap recursive self-improvement, the case where a model designs its own successor. Four outside advisers worked on it, among them Alondra Nelson, a former acting director of the White House science office, alongside the Governor's Office of Emergency Services.
- **Why it matters:** This is the first state proposal to treat a model's ability to improve itself as something a government can cap. The shutdown power has no Australian or New Zealand analogue, and the report is written to be copied by other legislatures. It is advice rather than law: the two statutes it builds on set up verification bodies and an auditor registry, not standards. The timing is political. It follows the state's own suit against OpenAI by a day, and it argues that Washington has done nothing.
- **Source:** [Office of the Governor of California — California releases next steps on mandatory safety standards and oversight](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/11/18/governor-newsoms-nation-leading-ai-safety-framework-is-getting-even-stronger-as-california-releases-next-steps-on-mandatory-safety-standards-and-oversight/) &middot; [Government Operations Agency — AI Safety in 2026 and Beyond (report, PDF)](https://www.govops.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2026/11/FINAL_-AI-Safety-in-2026-and-Beyond-v2.pdf)
- **Impact:** Elevated
- **Obligation:** Proposed
- **Obligation ref:** California SB 53 (Transparency in Frontier AI Act, 2025) — safety-framework disclosure and critical-incident reporting; SB 813 (2026) — independent verification organisations; AB 1405 (2026) — AI auditor registry. The November report recommends new statutory powers, including emergency shutdown and an RSI cap; none is enacted.
- **Evidence:** Confirmed
- **Reliability:** Tier 1/4 — Primary (the Governor's release and the agency report, both published in full; the recommendations are advisory, no bill has been introduced, and the report's framing is the administration's own)
- **Verification:** Verified
- **Date:** 2026-11-18

## Research & evidence

### 1. Three models hunting the same code agreed on one flaw in 121

- **Summary:** **Echo**, a software supply-chain firm, ran Claude Mythos 5.1, GPT-5.6 Sol and Z.ai's GLM-5.3 against the same three open-source projects using one hunting framework. Nine runs produced 446 findings, which filtered to 121 distinct potential issues. Only one was found by all three models, and 100 were found by a single model. The models rated 45 per cent of the findings high or critical; after proof-of-concept testing, 26 per cent held. Run costs ranged from $22 for GLM-5.3 to $303 for Mythos. Echo will not name the projects until maintainers have been notified.
- **Why it matters:** If models disagree this widely, a defender who standardises on one is blind to what the others see, and an attacker running an open-weight model sees something else again. That argues for several models plus validation, which is the service Echo sells, so read the finding with that in mind. The second number is the more useful one: more than half the high-severity findings did not survive testing. For an Australian maintainer or security team, discovery is becoming cheap and triage is not.
- **Source:** [Echo — Three leading AI models hunted the same code for vulnerabilities and agreed on just 1 in 121](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/11/18/3405402/0/en/three-leading-ai-models-hunted-the-same-code-for-vulnerabilities-and-agreed-on-just-1-in-121.html) &middot; [Echo — Frontier research: methodology and data](https://echo.ai/frontier-research)
- **Impact:** Guarded
- **Obligation:** None
- **Obligation ref:** None
- **Evidence:** Other
- **Reliability:** Tier 3/4 — Moderate (a vendor's controlled comparison with method and costs published; not peer-reviewed, the projects are unnamed, and the firm sells the validation step it recommends)
- **Verification:** Verified
- **Date:** 2026-11-18

## AI security & agentic risk

### 1. A hijacked agent framework stole cloud keys on every Python start

- **Summary:** A malicious build of **Microsoft**'s `agent-framework` package reached PyPI at 20:35 UTC on 17 November, and a second version followed 33 minutes later. Both were gone within the hour. Each dropped a `.pth` file, which Python executes at interpreter start, so the stealer ran even when the package was never imported. It collected environment variables, Azure managed-identity tokens, Azure DevOps and GitHub Actions OIDC tokens, and credentials for npm, pip, Docker and Hugging Face. Wiz traced the publish to a compromised Microsoft code-signing certificate, and read the attacker's own progress reports on a public webhook.
- **Why it matters:** The root cause is the part that generalises: a stolen client certificate for the pipeline that signs and publishes a package, not a compromise of the source repository. Any organisation that trusts a vendor's release pipeline inherits that pipeline's key custody. Wiz counted 17 requests carrying three victims' data, and notes that tokens taken this way stay valid for up to 24 hours. Pinning the last clean version, 1.26.0, and rotating credentials is the response. Checking whether your code imports the framework is not, because the hook fires either way.
- **Source:** [Wiz — Microsoft agent-framework compromised: credential stealer targets Azure and CI pipelines](https://www.wiz.io/blog/malicious-version-of-microsofts-agent-framework-published-to-pypi)
- **Impact:** Elevated
- **Obligation:** None
- **Obligation ref:** None
- **Evidence:** Confirmed
- **Reliability:** Tier 2/4 — High (Wiz observed the publish, the removal and the attacker's exfiltration channel; Microsoft has not published its own account, the attacker is unidentified, and how the certificate was obtained is unknown)
- **Verification:** Verified
- **Date:** 2026-11-18

### 2. Microsoft gave every agent a login and a manager

- **Summary:** At Ignite on 17 November **Microsoft** said more than 50 million agents have been registered in Agent 365 since its May launch. The service now pulls agents from Salesforce Agentforce, Amazon Bedrock, Google Vertex AI, Databricks, Snowflake, OpenAI and Anthropic's Claude Managed Agents into one registry. Each connected agent receives a Microsoft Entra Agent ID, so its sign-ins and actions are logged against the agent rather than the employee who triggered it. Defender has discovered more than 60 local agents, including Claude Code and GitHub Copilot CLI, and each gets its own identity. Copilot Autopilot, a continuous agent, runs under an Entra identity with an assigned manager. Most of the capabilities are in preview.
- **Why it matters:** Agent identity is the control that makes the rest auditable, and it is arriving as a vendor product rather than an agreed standard. Two consequences follow. A coding agent running on a laptop becomes an identity that IT can revoke without touching the developer's own access. And an agent built by a competitor now lives inside Microsoft's identity system, so the platform that hosts it can suspend it. The Transparency Initiative with 40 organisations and NIST's agent standards work is the part aimed at making that portable, and it is the part with no timeline.
- **Source:** [Microsoft — Ignite 2026: Building the frontier ecosystem](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/11/17/ignite-2026-building-the-frontier-ecosystem/) &middot; [Microsoft — What's new in Microsoft Agent 365: Ignite 2026](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/agent-365-blog/whats-new-in-microsoft-agent-365-ignite-2026/4571753)
- **Impact:** Guarded
- **Obligation:** None
- **Obligation ref:** None
- **Evidence:** Confirmed
- **Reliability:** Tier 1/4 — Primary (vendor, self-reported: the registry count, the partner list and which features are preview are Microsoft's own statements, and preview capabilities can change)
- **Verification:** Verified
- **Date:** 2026-11-17

## Frontier models & capability claims

### 1. Genie 4 lets you rewrite a world while you are inside it

- **Summary:** **Google DeepMind** released Genie 4 to Google AI Ultra subscribers on 18 November. It generates an interactive world from a prompt and accepts new prompts mid-session, so weather, characters and action can change while the world runs. It adds generated audio that adapts to the scene, including dialogue. The same release stretches Genie 3 sessions to three minutes and links worlds into sequences. A new agent builds environments through conversation, and a gallery lets users publish and remix them. Google still describes both models as research.
- **Why it matters:** The quality claims are unverified and the framing is the interesting part. A world model that accepts edits in real time is a training environment for agents rather than a game, and simulated environments are where agentic and embodied systems learn. One that responds to language moves the work from level design to reward design. Google's own list of open problems, camera latency, physical interaction and scene persistence, is the honest part of the release. Every output carries SynthID watermarking, which settles provenance at the source.
- **Source:** [Google — Genie 4 is now available](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/genie-4-now-available-project-genie/)
- **Impact:** Guarded
- **Obligation:** None
- **Obligation ref:** None
- **Capability:** Frontier
- **Evidence:** Vendor claim
- **Reliability:** Tier 1/4 — Primary (vendor, self-reported: the features are live for subscribers, while every claim about fidelity, physics and realism is Google's own)
- **Verification:** Verified
- **Date:** 2026-11-18
