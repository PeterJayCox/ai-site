# Executive Summary

**Google put its strongest model behind a cyber-defender gate.** Google DeepMind announced Gemini 4 Carbon on 28 October, two weeks after Gemini 4 Argon. Carbon ranks first on Google's own cybersecurity benchmarks, scoring 88.2 per cent on SEC-bench Pro for finding vulnerabilities, 54.8 per cent on ExploitGym for exploiting them, and 71.0 per cent on CWE-bench for patching them. It is not on general sale. Access opens this week to a small group of cyber defenders, then to trusted partners through Google's Fairwind Program, with no date given for developers or consumers.

**Anthropic spent the window building its position in the Pacific.** On 28 October the company opened a Singapore office and signed a memorandum of understanding with the Ministry of Digital Development and Information. The MOU covers AI safety, security and model evaluation. The same day it launched the Claude Frontier Academy, a US$100 million programme to train 10,000 engineers by the end of 2027. The first cohorts include the Commonwealth Bank of Australia.

**The enterprise tier kept absorbing frontier models.** OpenAI and ServiceNow expanded their partnership on 28 October. OpenAI's models and Codex move into ServiceNow's application-development, security and voice products. In the cybersecurity piece, the two companies will pair OpenAI's models with ServiceNow's asset and identity data to investigate vulnerabilities and coordinate remediation.

**Three things follow.** The capability being sold is close to the capability the threat picture describes. Google's cyber figures are its own, run without guardrails, and access is gated by a programme Google controls. Anthropic is now selling training as a product, with an Australian bank that is also its customer in the first cohort. And the liability question is unsettled: Anthropic told the US securities regulator that no single insurer, and possibly the whole industry, may be able to cover a catastrophic AI incident.

**The arc.** Expect the frontier-lab contest to move into the cyber-defence lane, where the buyer is a security team rather than a consumer. Expect the talent layer to become a product in its own right. And expect insurers, not regulators, to define the practical limits of AI deployment first.

## Frontier models & capability claims

### 1. Google put its strongest model behind a cyber-defender gate, two weeks after the last one

- **Summary:** **Google DeepMind** announced **Gemini 4 Carbon** on 28 October, calling it its strongest model yet for cybersecurity and long-horizon work. The company says Carbon ranks first on an updated Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at 59. It reports state-of-the-art scores on three external cybersecurity benchmarks, all run without guardrails. Carbon scores 88.2 per cent on SEC-bench Pro for finding vulnerabilities, 54.8 per cent on ExploitGym for validating which are exploitable, and 71.0 per cent on CWE-bench for patching them. Google says Carbon writes secure code more than twice as often as Argon on internal tests, and that its Chrome team found it more than ten times better at producing patches than another lab's frontier model. Carbon is not generally available. Access begins this week for a subset of cyber defenders, then trusted partners through the Fairwind Program, with no date for developers or consumers. It launches at Argon's introductory price of US$2 per million input tokens and US$10 per million output tokens, rising to US$4 and US$20 after an unspecified period.
- **Why it matters:** The model that finds and exploits vulnerabilities is the same class of capability the ASD's threat report described a day earlier. Google's numbers are its own, run without guardrails, and the release gate is a programme Google controls. The price and the cyber framing together make Carbon the clearest test yet of whether defensive AI can be restricted to defenders in practice.

### 2. DeepMind's Genie 4 simulates a world with sound and dialogue

- **Summary:** **Google DeepMind** announced **Genie 4** on 27 October, a world model that generates interactive, photorealistic environments with synchronised audio. A user steers a scene with keyboard controls and text prompts, and Gemini models adapt the prompt to the surrounding scene. Genie 4 builds on capabilities from the Gemini family: Gemini Omni anchors the simulation's appearance and behaviour, and other Gemini models keep it coherent. DeepMind says audio-like music, ambient noise and interactive dialogue are generated with the world at the same time. The model has started as a research preview for trusted testers, ahead of a wider release to Google AI Ultra subscribers and Gemini API partners.
- **Why it matters:** It moves generative AI from producing content to producing a responsive environment, which is the training substrate for robots and agents. The same capability is what makes synthetic worlds hard to separate from real footage, a provenance problem no AUNZ regulator has addressed. It is a research preview, and Google has published no independent evaluation.
- **Source:** [Google DeepMind — Genie 4: A new generation of world simulation](https://deepmind.google/blog/genie-4-a-new-generation-of-world-simulation/)

## Market & geopolitics

### 1. Anthropic opened a Singapore office and signed an AI-safety MOU with the government

- **Summary:** **Anthropic** opened a Singapore office on 28 October, its fifth in the Asia-Pacific region, and signed a memorandum of understanding with the **Singapore Ministry of Digital Development and Information**. The MOU sets out cooperation on AI safety, security and model evaluation, research on AI adoption, and responsible use across the public and private sectors. Anthropic co-founder **Ben Mann** signed with the ministry's permanent secretary, Kai Fong Chng, on the sidelines of Singapore International Cyber Week. The company says it will hold technical exchanges on model evaluation, testing standards and incident response. It also plans to share research and data with the government, including from its Anthropic Economic Index. Singapore ranks second of 121 countries in Claude usage relative to population.
- **Why it matters:** A government is handing a frontier lab both the market and the safety relationship at once, which is the pattern AUNZ jurisdictions will be offered. Singapore is further along than Australia on model evaluation, so the terms it agrees become a drafting template. The instrument is a memorandum, not a statute, and it obliges nobody outside the two signatories.

### 2. OpenAI and ServiceNow will put models and Codex into the workflow layer enterprises already run

- **Summary:** **OpenAI** and **ServiceNow** expanded their partnership on 28 October. ServiceNow will bring OpenAI models and agents into products spanning development, security and voice, and connect its workflows to ChatGPT and Codex. OpenAI says ServiceNow's AI Workflow Factory will gain a Codex integration, and that OpenAI models will power two further development tools, Build Agent and Autonomous Engineer. A joint security product will combine OpenAI's cybersecurity models with ServiceNow's asset, identity and business data, so teams can investigate vulnerabilities and prioritise remediation. ServiceNow plans to give ChatGPT Work and Codex to its whole workforce, and OpenAI is already running ServiceNow workflows in its own operations.
- **Why it matters:** The workflow system is where an enterprise's permissions, assets and audit trail already live. Wiring frontier models into it decides whether agents act inside governed systems or beside them. That is the layer an AUNZ bank or agency has to sign off, and where the security review happens.

### 3. Anthropic will spend US$100 million training 10,000 engineers inside its customers

- **Summary:** **Anthropic** launched the **Claude Frontier Academy** on 28 October, backing it with a US$100 million commitment. The academy aims to train **10,000** Frontier Deployed Engineers by the end of 2027. The first cohorts draw engineers from Accenture, Bain, Capgemini, Deloitte, McKinsey, Morgan Stanley, Novo Nordisk and the **Commonwealth Bank of Australia**. The first programme runs a short in-person course, then a twelve-week residency in which each engineer leads a real Claude deployment at their own organisation, with assessment at the end. Cohorts are running in San Francisco, New York and London, and the first full credentials are expected in early 2027. Anthropic's partner network already carries more than 175,000 certifications across 46,000 firms.
- **Why it matters:** The scarce input in enterprise AI is people who can take a use case to production, and Anthropic is now selling that as a product rather than leaving partners to find it. It is also a form of lock-in, because the engineers are trained on Claude first. For AUNZ enterprises, the CBA cohort is a live example of a bank building capability through a vendor's academy rather than a university.

### 4. Anthropic told the SEC that no insurer may be able to cover a catastrophic AI incident

- **Summary:** **Anthropic's** registration statement warns that the insurance industry may not be able to cover the liabilities its models could create. **CNBC** reported the passage on 28 October. The filing states that claims from unintended model behaviour could produce substantial liability. It says its policies may not cover that liability, and that "no single insurer, and potentially the insurance industry in the aggregate, may have sufficient capacity or expertise" to underwrite a catastrophic AI incident. It adds that the market for AI-specific risk is nascent and that cover may be unavailable on commercial terms. Insurance analysts told CNBC that cyber policies cover AI-related data and security losses but almost always exclude bodily injury. One Gartner analyst estimates deaths involving AI could pass 2,000 worldwide by the end of 2026.
- **Why it matters:** It names the boundary that decides what actually gets deployed. A capability that cannot be insured is one an AUNZ board cannot approve, whatever a regulator permits. Expect exclusions, low sub-limits and human-review requirements, and expect a vendor's own product to sit inside the causal chain.
- **Source:** [CNBC — Anthropic warns of uninsurable AI risks. It may not be only company](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/28/anthropic-ipo-ai-risks-insurance.html) &middot; [SEC — Anthropic PBC registration statement (Form S-1)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm)
