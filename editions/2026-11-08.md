## AI security & agentic risk

### 1. Three men were charged over $129 million stolen with cloned voices

- **Summary:** The **US Department of Justice** unsealed an indictment on 5 November against three men accused of using AI to impersonate executives and lawyers. Jacob Soall, Sharon Hazan and Guy Sheetrit are alleged to have mimicked the voices and images of chief executives on calls to employees. They then posed as lawyers at large international firms and told the employees to wire funds for a confidential transaction. The scheme took more than $129 million between 2024 and 2026. Victims included two hospital systems, a Rhode Island bank and a British financial technology company that lost $11.6 million. The men were arrested in Spain and the United Kingdom. Extradition requests are pending.
- **Why it matters:** The control that failed here was not technical. An employee received a payment instruction and acted on it because the voice and the face were right. Australia's privacy framework asks for reasonable security of personal information under APP 11, and no state or federal offence is specific to voice cloning. Deepfake fraud therefore lands on ordinary payment controls. A call-back to a number already on file, and dual authorisation above a threshold, still stop it.
- **Source:** [US Department of Justice — Three foreign nationals arrested and charged with scheme to use super intelligence to defraud companies of $129 million](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/three-foreign-nationals-arrested-and-charged-scheme-use-super-intelligence-defraud) &middot; [The Guardian — Three men charged in US with using AI to impersonate CEOs and steal millions](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/nov/06/ai-ceo-impersonation-theft)

## Research & evidence

### 1. OpenAI built a benchmark for the messy end of laboratory science

- **Summary:** **OpenAI** published BioBasicsBench on 6 November, a test of 100 routine wet-lab tasks built with the biotech firm Ligo. The tasks use raw experimental evidence: gel and blot images, plate photographs, sequencing reads and instrument exports. An agent has to read the data, spot a failed control and say when the evidence cannot support a conclusion. At the highest reasoning setting, GPT-6 Astra scored 66.7 per cent of available credit against 43.8 per cent for GPT-5.6 Sol. OpenAI also published the failures. Astra overcounted colonies on one plate by about 12 per cent, and marked empty agar as a colony.
- **Why it matters:** Benchmarks drawn from published papers flatter models, because a paper's figures are already the clean ones. Bench data is where an agent will actually be pointed, and where a misread costs an experiment. The honest reading for AUNZ labs is that agent-assisted analysis is improving fast and is not yet dependable at the routine end. The biosecurity implication runs the same way, which is why OpenAI says it will keep tracking these tasks.
- **Source:** [OpenAI — Measuring how AI agents handle everyday wet-lab science](https://openai.com/index/measuring-how-ai-agents-handle-wet-lab-science/)
- **Impact:** Low
- **Obligation:** None
- **Obligation ref:** None
- **Capability:** Material
- **Evidence:** Vendor claim
- **Reliability:** Tier 1/4 — Primary (vendor, self-reported: OpenAI built the benchmark, graded its own models against it and publishes no independent replication)
- **Verification:** Verified
- **Date:** 2026-11-06

## Regulation & obligation

### 1. Washington and Moscow are working to stop binding rules on autonomous weapons

- **Summary:** **Russia and the United States** are leading a group of states trying to stop a United Nations conference from authorising negotiations on a legally binding instrument for autonomous weapons. The review conference of the certain conventional weapons convention runs in Geneva from 16 to 20 November, with 128 countries due to attend. A coalition of 50 states led by Ireland called on 22 October for a mandate covering three negotiating sessions in 2027. Russia, the US and Israel want talks to continue on voluntary measures instead. The United Kingdom did not join the Irish statement. The International Committee of the Red Cross said on 5 November that the time had come to negotiate.
- **Why it matters:** This is the governance gap deciding whether to close. The instrument in question would set limits on weapons that select and engage a target without a human referral. Australia is a party to the convention, so its delegation will have to take a position on the mandate in Geneva. A conference that settles for more voluntary discussion leaves the ADF and its partners operating to national policy alone, which is where the rules already sit.
- **Source:** [The Guardian — Russia and US seek to block plan for binding rules on autonomous weapons](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/nov/06/autonomous-weapons-ai-russia-us-binding-rules)
- **Impact:** Elevated
- **Obligation:** Proposed
- **Obligation ref:** Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons — review conference mandate under Article 8; consensus elements of a draft protocol exist, with no operative provision yet
- **Evidence:** Confirmed
- **Reliability:** Tier 2/4 — High (a masthead report quoting named campaigners, the ICRC and government positions; the conference outcome is not yet known)

Continuity risk at that point is a delivery risk, not a reputational one. AUNZ organisations waiting on guidance should expect the timetable to rest on a smaller group of people for the next few months.

## Infrastructure & compute

### 1. Advanced chip packaging became the bottleneck the buildout cannot route around

- **Summary:** **Advanced packaging** is emerging as the constraint on AI chip supply, according to reporting from Taipei on 6 November. Revenue from packaging is forecast to overtake revenue from conventional chipmaking for the first time in 2026, with capital spending near US$28 billion, according to the research firm Yole Group. TSMC dominates the most advanced technique, chip-on-wafer-on-substrate, and its capacity limits have pushed buyers to other suppliers. Nvidia is providing billions of dollars to expand packaging capacity at Siliconware Precision Industries. Taiwan's Powertech plans a US$400 million packaging joint venture with Broadcom in Singapore, with production from 2028.
- **Why it matters:** Every AUNZ capacity forecast assumes chips arrive. The constraint has moved downstream from lithography to the step where processors and memory are joined, and that step sits in a handful of Taiwanese plants. Whoever holds packaging capacity sets the schedule for everyone else. For buyers, the contract that matters is now a capacity agreement with a named supplier rather than a compute price.
- **Source:** [Reuters — AI boom turns chip packaging into the next battleground for semiconductors](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/ai-boom-turns-chip-packaging-into-next-battleground-semiconductors-2026-11-06/)
