## Regulation & obligation

### 1. Australia and China agreed to open an AI dialogue

- **Summary:** The commitment is one line, paragraph eight of the Australia–China Statement on Joint Outcomes. It reads: "The two sides agreed on the establishment of an AI dialogue, to exchange respective views on AI safety, governance and application." The **Prime Minister** published the statement on Saturday 21 November. It followed the Annual Leaders' Meeting that Li Qiang and Anthony Albanese convened in Beijing on 20 November. Paragraph six also welcomes the APEC leaders' statement from Shenzhen, including its outcomes on the digital economy and artificial intelligence. Neither government published terms of reference, a participant list, an agenda or a date for a first meeting.
- **Why it matters:** A dialogue is a channel, not a rule, and it commits neither side to anything enforceable. Its value is that it exists before Australia's AI Standards legislation reaches Parliament in 2027. Australian and New Zealand firms that build or buy AI from Chinese platforms now have a government channel through which to raise a safety question, which previously meant a regulator with no counterpart. Watch the second meeting, not the first: a dialogue that produces no published readout is a photograph opportunity.
- **Source:** [Prime Minister of Australia — Statement on Joint Outcomes of the Australia-China Annual Leaders' Meeting](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/statement-joint-outcomes-australia-china-annual-leaders-meeting-0)

## AI security & agentic risk

### 1. An AI framework found a zero-day on its own and used it twice

- **Summary:** **Amazon's** threat-intelligence team reported that Chinese-speaking operators pointed Cairn, an open-source autonomous AI penetration-testing framework, at universities in East Asia. The team found a publicly accessible server hosting an instance of the tool and could read its full operational history. Prompts were written in Simplified Chinese and timestamps were set to China Standard Time. Given only IP addresses and the goal of obtaining shell access, the agents planned, executed and adjusted their approach without the operator choosing each step. One attempt ran 148 actions over 16 hours. The one success was a path-traversal flaw in an open-source medical-imaging application with no advisory and no fix.
- **Why it matters:** Two things are new, and neither is the model. The first is autonomy: the agents worked through 30,000 credential combinations against 80 real user names and a failed Log4Shell attempt without a human deciding what to try next. The second is that the vulnerable software was itself an AI application in a clinical setting. Amazon assesses the flaw was a genuine zero-day, and the maintainers have patched it. An agent that goes from an address to a shell in minutes works faster than a security operations centre triages an alert, which is the sentence Australian and New Zealand hospital and university network owners should read twice.
- **Source:** [Amazon — Chinese-nexus actors' Cairn AI agents found a zero-day on their own](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/security/chinese-nexus-actors-cairn-ai-agents-found-a-zero-day-on-their-own/) &middot; [The Record — Amazon says Chinese hackers used Cairn AI tool to discover apparent zero-day during attack on university](https://therecord.media/amazon-research-cairn-ai-university-attacks-possible-zero-day)
- **Impact:** Elevated
- **Obligation:** None
- **Obligation ref:** None
- **Capability:** Material
- **Evidence:** Corroborated
- **Reliability:** Tier 1/4 — Primary (the cloud provider's own threat-intelligence unit, writing from an instance's captured history; Amazon does not name the universities, the country or the attackers, and the "high confidence" zero-day assessment is its own)
- **Verification:** Verified

### 2. Bell Canada says an "agentic" attacker took enterprise data

- **Summary:** **Bell Canada** confirmed that an "agentic threat actor" breached its systems on 17 November and stole enterprise customer data. The entry point was a pre-production environment of the carrier's Maximo system, which is used to order and provision telecommunications circuits. The data taken included business contact details, information in ticket titles, and some IP addresses of network elements. Bell told BleepingComputer it contained the incident and that customer services were unaffected. It is sharing indicators of compromise with enterprise customers and the Canadian Centre for Cyber Security, but has published none of them. It has not identified the attacker or said how many customers were affected.
- **Why it matters:** The word doing the work here is "agentic", and it is the victim's characterisation, not a finding. A carrier describing its own intrusion as machine-driven is a notable shift, because it tells customers what to defend against next. The missing piece is the one that would let them: indicators withheld from the public are withheld from every organisation that shares Bell's software. Australian and New Zealand carriers running the same enterprise provisioning suites should ask for those indicators directly, rather than assume a peer's incident stops at the border.
- **Source:** [BleepingComputer — Bell Canada says 'agentic' hacker stole enterprise customer data](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/bell-canada-says-agentic-hacker-stole-enterprise-customer-data/)

### 3. OpenAI closed the door on its own reasoning traces

- **Summary:** **OpenAI** published its defences against adversarial distillation on 20 November. The technique uses a frontier model's outputs to train a second model with similar capabilities and weaker safeguards. OpenAI's focus is the chain of thought, the intermediate steps a model writes on the way to an answer, which carry detail the final answer omits. It hardened those protections in GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol, and tested whether an attacker could recover reasoning from an earlier prompt. In an internal adaptive evaluation using automated red-teaming, the average attack success rate fell from 54.0 per cent against GPT-6 Astra to 0.2 per cent against GPT-6.2 Astra. Its own study found that students trained on exact reasoning traces outperformed those trained on summaries.
- **Why it matters:** The measurement is the argument for hiding reasoning, and it cuts against the industry's drift toward showing it. OpenAI's numbers say a summary teaches less than a transcript, including cyber capability transfer on tasks its own policy permits. That is why effort is now going into classifiers, API controls and campaign-level investigation rather than into the model's behaviour alone. The figure is the company's own internal evaluation of its own models, so treat it as a claim about direction rather than a validated rate. Anthropic, whose equivalent work OpenAI cites, reported the same advantage on a software benchmark.
- **Source:** [OpenAI — Combatting adversarial distillation](https://openai.com/index/combatting-adversarial-distillation)
- **Impact:** Guarded
- **Obligation:** None
- **Obligation ref:** None
- **Capability:** Incremental
- **Evidence:** Vendor claim
- **Reliability:** Tier 1/4 — Primary (vendor, self-reported: the safeguards are live and the study is published, but the 54.0 to 0.2 per cent comparison is OpenAI's internal adaptive evaluation of its own two model generations, with no external replication)
- **Verification:** Verified
- **Date:** 2026-11-20

## Research & evidence

### 1. A cheap adapter squeezed 16 points out of a 27-billion model

- **Summary:** **Hugging Face** published a reusable recipe for training an adapter to a specific agent harness on 20 November. The technique freezes the model's weights and adds a small RL-trained LoRA adapter, trained inside the harness exactly as it ships rather than against a framework's reimplementation of the agent loop. On Qwen3.8-27B running the pi harness on Terminal-Bench 2.1, the base model scored 39 per cent pass@1 at about 2,900 output tokens per task. With a custom adapter it scored 55 per cent at about 1,500 tokens. Three adapters for three harnesses were merged into one that runs unchanged across all of them.
- **Why it matters:** This is the cheapest capability gain in the stack, and it does not need a frontier lab. A 16-point jump with lower token use on a 27-billion-parameter open model means the harness, not just the model, is now a performance variable. For Australian and New Zealand teams running local or self-hosted agents, the practical read is that a generic model is leaving throughput on the table until it is tuned to the tool loop it actually runs. The artefacts are published and the recipe is reproducible, which is why this is carded at all.
- **Source:** [Hugging Face — Introducing harness adapters: adapt your favorite model to your harness to improve performance and cost](https://huggingface.co/blog/harness-adapters-at-scale)
- **Impact:** Low
- **Obligation:** None
- **Obligation ref:** None
- **Capability:** Incremental
- **Evidence:** Other
- **Reliability:** Tier 2/4 — High (an engineering report from the platform that served the training, with adapters, quantised weights, tasks and the plugin published; the benchmark numbers are the authors' own runs, and the post itself flags an open question about what the model actually learned)
- **Verification:** Verified
- **Date:** 2026-11-20
