## Infrastructure & compute

### 1. A stalled regulator pushed Australia's biggest outback AI data centre off schedule

- **Summary:** **Energy North** has scrapped the target start date for its Project Ares data centre in the Northern Territory, Reuters reported on 2 November. The developer had given a "first power" date of 28 June 2028 in regulatory filings four months ago. The Territory's environment regulator said on 7 October that it was suspending its seven-week deadline to decide what hurdles the project must clear. The 1-gigawatt facility would sit on a cattle ranch 683 kilometres from the nearest city, alongside a gas turbine, a solar farm and an airstrip. The regulator warned of ten impacts, from the strain on water systems to the disruption of Aboriginal sacred sites.
- **Why it matters:** It is the largest Australian AI data centre to slip so far, which shows the binding constraint is permitting rather than capital. The regulator paused because the federal government is still drafting policy expectations for data centres and AI infrastructure, so the project is waiting on a national framework that does not yet exist. For an AUNZ developer, timetable risk now sits in the regulator's queue, not the boardroom.

## Frontier models & capability claims

### 1. DeepSeek open-sourced a coding agent built to watch a team's chat

- **Summary:** **DeepSeek** released Super-Agent, an experimental coding agent, on 1 November. The Chinese lab published the code under the name dsh-superagent with an MIT licence. The agent works on one GitHub repository and can also listen to Feishu, a workplace chat app. When an approved person asks it for work, it opens its own session and replies in the original thread, for example with a pull request. DeepSeek calls it a research prototype and "not an official DeepSeek product". Bloomberg reported the release is aimed at Anthropic's Claude Code.
- **Why it matters:** It is the open-weight play applied to agents: DeepSeek is commoditising the coding assistant that US labs sell. The stated design goal, to take as little human attention as possible, is also the risk, because an agent acting for a whole team can spend that attention rather than save it. An AUNZ enterprise now has a free, inspectable coding agent, with no support and a caution to review the code first.

## Market & geopolitics

### 1. Apple confirmed Apple Intelligence in China, powered by Alibaba's Qwen

- **Summary:** **Apple** confirmed on 31 October that Apple Intelligence will launch in China, with Alibaba's Qwen model built in. The first features arrive in a developer beta from 4 November, then publicly in December with iOS 27.3, Apple said in a post on its Chinese newsroom. Qwen will run in Siri, Writing Tools and Visual Intelligence. The rollout includes Live Translation, Genmoji and notification summaries. Apple said "Siri AI" and its other next-generation features are not part of the first release. Chinese approval had kept Apple Intelligence out of the mainland since 2024.
- **Why it matters:** It is the template for how a US model vendor enters a market that will not take an American model directly: pair with a local one and ship what is approved. For AUNZ users it is mostly a signal, but it reframes the sovereignty debate, where a local model is now the price of entry rather than a preference. Australia's own AI procurement will be asked the same question.
- **Source:** [The Apple Post — Apple Intelligence finally coming to China in December with iOS 27.3](https://www.theapplepost.com/2026/10/31/73757/apple-intelligence-finally-coming-to-china-in-december-with-ios-27-3/) &middot; [Apple Newsroom (China) — Apple Intelligence features are coming to users in China](https://www.apple.com.cn/newsroom/2026/10/apple-intelligence-features-are-coming-to-users-in-china/)

## Regulation & obligation

### 1. New York opened registration for frontier AI developers under the RAISE Act

- **Summary:** **New York** began registering large frontier AI developers with the state from 1 November, under the Responsible AI Safety and Education (RAISE) Act. Governor Kathy Hochul announced the step on 21 September. Developers above the law's revenue and training-compute thresholds must register with the new Office of Digital Innovation, Governance, Integrity and Trust, or DIGIT, inside the Department of Financial Services. From January 2027 they must meet the safety standards and report critical incidents within 72 hours. The governor said she is exploring further steps.
- **Why it matters:** It is the first US state registration regime for frontier developers, and it lands while federal action stalls. The duty is thin beside the EU's, but it is enforceable, which makes it the reference point other states will copy. For an AUNZ board it is a reminder that the operating rule for a global model is now set by the strictest jurisdiction it serves.
- **Source:** [New York State Department of Financial Services — Governor Hochul announces next steps to regulate major AI developers](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/reports_and_publications/press_releases/pr20260921) &middot; [Politico — New York moves forward with implementing landmark AI legislation](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/09/21/new-york-implementing-ai-legislation-01085775)
- **Impact:** Guarded
