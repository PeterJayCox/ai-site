2. The badge row — the core fix

Today six filled pills where red, green and violet each mean several unrelated things, and the reliability note is a sentence in a pill that outweighs the headline.

Ukraine struck a third Yandex data centre in four days, halting the compute behind Russia's AI effort

Overnight into Sunday 11 October, Ukrainian drones struck Yandex's data centre in the Vladimir region, east of Moscow.

Al Jazeera — Russia's Yandex says data centre affected by drone attack Impact: Elevated Obligation: Signalled Evidence: Confirmed Tier 2/4 — High (on-record statement from a government task force, reported as an exclusive; the State Department's account carried by a wire report the following day) Verified 2026-10-10

Before. Six chips, three hues, and the tier note wraps to two lines and dominates the row. Red here means "confirmed", the green "verified" — different axes, and a reader has no way to learn that from the page.

After. Colour now means exactly one thing — severity — and only the Impact segment carries it. The other axes are monochrome with a leading glyph, so they read as metadata instead of alarms. The tier rationale becomes a short qualifier, and the full sentence moves to a title tooltip and the key table. The card top rule is the theme's identity strip, deliberately outside the badge colour language.

3. One colour scale, five steps

Impact is the only semantically coloured axis. Everything categorical (theme, obligation, evidence kind, capability, tier) is grey. Nothing reuses a severity hue.

4. Other fixes, no mockup needed

Landing: retitle "What it will be" → "What it is"; move "Why the claim-type badge matters" and "Status" to an About page; add a live one-card preview of the latest edition so the landing shows the product rather than describing it.

Editions: a sticky section rail (six theme anchors) and prev/next edition links on the daily page; a timeline or sparkline of stories-per-day on /daily/ so the archive shows its shape.

Exec summary: split the long "Top stories" paragraph into a one-line lead plus a tight bulleted readout of the rest, so the summary is scannable before the cards.

Key table: drop it to the About page or collapse it behind a "how to read this" toggle — once the colour language is self-evident, an always-open legend is redundant.
