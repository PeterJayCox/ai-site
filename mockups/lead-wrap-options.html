Two measures in one card

A model marketplace pulled three providers over logged prompts

OpenRouter off-boarded three inference providers and removed 125 endpoints from its Zero Data Retention eligibility list. The marketplace announced the action on 22 November, after Dragos researchers ran canary-token prompts against hosted models from July to November. Their tests found resellers logging prompts, and providers ignoring no-collection flags. OpenRouter says no breach touched its own systems. Three providers have posted notices: Together, which admitted prompt leakage for requests made in June and July; Novita, on MiniMax requests; and Phala, in a preliminary incident report.

Why it matters

A retention badge on a model marketplace is a promise, and nothing was auditing it. Dragos only found this by planting tokens and watching where they surfaced. Any team sending proprietary code or customer data through a reseller should treat the label as unverified, and check which provider actually serves its endpoint. Australian and New Zealand firms that buy inference through aggregators rather than direct now have a documented failure mode in that chain.

Option A

One measure, aligned

The outright fix, and the smallest: both prose blocks get the one 530px right edge, so the judgement pulls back 76px and sets at ~66 characters instead of 77. Add text-wrap:pretty and the last line of each block stops being an orphan. Nothing is restructured, no markup changes, and the same rule fixes the cards inside the editions.

Trade-off: it does not reclaim the empty width — the prose still ends 429px short of the card's right edge, next to chips that run full width. It makes the wrap look deliberate instead of broken, and it fixes a real bug, but the card still reads narrow in a wide field. I would ship this regardless of which layout you choose.

Option B

Two-column standfirst

Newspaper setting: the news runs as two balanced columns of about 60 characters under the full-width headline, and the judgement drops out of the prose into a band beneath a hairline. The whole story is on screen at once, and the card reads as a page rather than a ribbon down the middle of one.

Trade-off: a column break lands mid-sentence by nature, so it needs orphans/widows tuning and a hard check at 720px, where the columns collapse. Highest visual payoff; also the fussiest to keep right as story lengths vary.
