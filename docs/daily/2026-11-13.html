Regulation & obligation 2 stories

1

Nonprofits sued to halt frontier model development outright

Four nonprofit groups sued OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta and SpaceXAI on 12 November. The complaint was filed in the Superior Court of California in San Francisco. It asks the court to bar further development of the most advanced models, including ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude. The groups are the Irreplaceable Education Fund, Public Citizen, Heat Initiative and The Alliance for Secure AI. They plead California's unfair competition law, arguing the products create a public nuisance. The complaint quotes the companies' own executives on catastrophic risk, including Anthropic's Dario Amodei and SpaceX's Elon Musk. It also cites recent incidents in which models escaped testing environments and attacked government systems. No hearing date has been set.

The theory is the news. It converts a company's own safety warnings into evidence of a defective product, which is why the complaint can quote a chief executive rather than commission research. A model release is normally a private commercial decision. This asks a state court to treat it as an act requiring permission. For AUNZ buyers, an injunction would land on the same quarterly release cadence the government's proposed independent-evaluation duty assumes.

2

The Finance secretary warned agencies off buying their own AI tools

Matt Yannopoulos runs the Department of Finance, and on 12 November he told federal agencies that big spending on their own AI tools would draw scrutiny. He spoke at the Digital Transformation Agency's AI event. GovAI, run by Finance, offers access to about 60 models, including ChatGPT and Claude. Only 33 of the 196 federal agencies have so far opted to trial it. The warning was reported by InnovationAus, which attended the event. No transcript of the remarks has been published.

Two numbers carry it: 33 of 196, and 60 models behind one gateway. The Commonwealth built GovAI so that public-service AI use sits inside a single procurement, a single set of terms and a single data boundary. A department that buys elsewhere steps outside that perimeter and out of Finance's visibility. For vendors, the addressable direct federal market is the 163 agencies that have not signed up. GovAI is also the reference implementation other Australian jurisdictions copy, which is why a procurement warning is worth recording as governance news.

Research & evidence 1 story

3

DeepMind moved its genome model from tissues to single cells

Google DeepMind published AlphaGenome 2 on 11 November. It is a DNA sequence model that predicts how a genetic variant changes gene expression in a named cell type. The company says it finds nearly twice as many expression-changing variants as its previous model. It was trained on more than 100 million cell profiles across 68 tissues. Researchers can name a cell type, or supply one derived from their own single-cell experiment. The model is free for non-commercial research through the AlphaGenome API, and in private preview for commercial work. DeepMind states it is for research only and has not been validated for clinical or diagnostic use.

The step from tissue averages to single cells is what makes a variant effect useful to a geneticist. A signal that vanishes when averaged across a tissue is precisely the one a rare-disease team is hunting. The claim is the vendor's and the paper has not been replicated. The access tier is the practical point for AUNZ institutions: a free API puts a cell-type model inside reach of a university lab with no compute budget.

AI security & agentic risk 1 story

4

Microsoft backdoored an agent model by editing seven neurons

Microsoft Research published a technique it calls Lazarus late on 10 November, US time. It turns a capable open-weight model into a backdoor by reprogramming fewer than ten rarely firing neurons. No retraining and no poisoned dataset are needed; write access to a model checkpoint is enough. The edited model behaves normally and passes standard evaluations. When a chosen trigger appears, it emits a command the agent executes with whatever permissions it already holds. Editing seven of more than 12,000 neurons in one eight-billion-parameter model produced the target command in at least 98.8 per cent of triggered sessions. Microsoft says it has seen no threat-actor use of the method.

The attack cost is the story. Poisoning training data needs a fine-tune and a GPU budget; editing a checkpoint needs one compromised account or pipeline. That moves the threat from the model publisher to anyone in the distribution path, including a mirror, a quantiser or an inference host. Microsoft's own conclusion is that no scan can prove a model clean, and that diffing weights against the publisher's declared base model is the strongest signal available. For AUNZ organisations running open-weight models, that makes a signed hash of the deployed weights the control to hold, not a behavioural test.

Frontier models & capability claims 1 story

5

Google put a cheaper Gemini 4 model into Search for everyone

Google released Gemini 4 Hydrogen on 12 November. It sits below the frontier Carbon and Argon models as the fast, cheap member of the family. Google says it scores 51 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at its default thinking level, against 50 for Argon. It costs $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output, flat across a one-million-token context. The company claims tasks finish 2.5 times faster than Gemini 3.8 Flash, with Terminal-Bench 4.1 rising from 18.2 to 57 per cent. The model shipped in the Gemini API, AI Studio, the Gemini app and paid Antigravity tiers, and in Search for all users worldwide.

The flat price across a million-token context is the consequential line. Long-context work has been priced by the token, so an agent reading a whole repository or case file pays for the read every time. Flat pricing changes which of those workloads are economic. The benchmark comparisons are Google's own, run on suites its partners publish, so treat the ranking as a claim. The Search deployment is the part with reach: it puts a Gemini 4 model in front of users who never chose a model. That is also the part an Australian regulator would reach first, since the proposed digital duty of care is written to cover AI services as well as platforms.

Infrastructure & compute 1 story

6

CDC refiled for a 1.4-gigawatt campus in western Sydney

CDC filed a revised application with the New South Wales government this month for a data centre campus at Kemps Creek. The filing covers six two-storey buildings totalling 1.4 gigawatts, plus an on-site substation, on 117.5 hectares. It replaces an August application for a single 144-megawatt building. The company describes the new filing as the entire campus under one approval rather than a first stage. CDC operates or is building more than 600 megawatts across Sydney, Melbourne, Canberra and Auckland, including 70 megawatts in New Zealand. Its development pipeline runs to a further 1.6 gigawatts by 2034. The application sits on the NSW major projects portal.

One campus at 1.4 gigawatts is more than twice everything the company has in operation and development across four cities. That is the scale now arriving at planning authorities one project at a time, which is the gap the NSW upper-house inquiry identified. The federal data centre expectations published this year would have a project this size underwrite new firm generation and pay its full connection cost. Whether the grid in that corridor can carry it is a question the application does not answer, and the answer will set the timetable for the AI capacity Australian buyers are being sold.

Market & geopolitics 1 story

7

Nvidia's board removed Jensen Huang and installed the finance chief

Nvidia's board announced on 12 November that Jensen Huang had resigned as president, chief executive and a director, effective immediately. Chief financial officer Colette Kress is interim chief executive. Former Synopsys chief executive Harvey Jones becomes the company's first board chair. The board met the previous night, after the New York Times reported a second extramarital affair, with an executive who reported to Huang. The first involved Alice Huang, a Chinese businesswoman US intelligence suspects of spying for Beijing. Nvidia shares fell as much as 12 per cent before closing down about 4.8 per cent. AMD and Broadcom each rose about 6 per cent. Huang, who holds a 3.61 per cent stake, also leaves the board.

The company is the systemically important node of the AI buildout and it now has an interim finance chief running operations. Nvidia has spent two years recycling its cash into the labs and neoclouds that buy its chips, and the governance question is who signs those commitments before a permanent appointment. The second-order effect is policy. Huang argued for continued access to the Chinese market while Congress moved the other way, and the House Speaker says consensus is building to limit chip exports. AUNZ buyers of GPU capacity are exposed to both legs of that, the price of the hardware and the export rule that decides where it can go.

Key to this page how to read it

Impact — the only coloured axis

How far the risk, obligation or capability position moves. Impact is not harm: half of what this digest carries — a model release, a research result, a funding round — is not a harm at all. The band is driven by precedent, blast radius, reversibility, structural change and sector criticality, not by a severity dictionary borrowed from cyber incidents.

Obligation — does it bind an AUNZ organisation?

Rendered only when the band is not None; a pill reading “Obligation: None” on every card is noise. Proposed means text exists; Signalled means a minister or a government has said it will — a two-stage ministerial statement stays Signalled. Where an obligation is claimed, the card names the instrument and its operative provision.

Evidence — how established is the claim?

Orthogonal to verification, and the reason this product exists separately from the cyber digest: an AI capability claim usually cannot be verified at all. A vendor testifying about its own model is Verified (they did say it) and simultaneously a Vendor claim (the capability is unreplicated). Both badges render.

Three rules that shape what appears

Freshness is a state-change test, not a date test. An item qualifies when something moved — a model shipped, a rule commenced, a vulnerability was exploited, a subpoena was served, a paper published. The window is 48 hours, 72 across a weekend bridge, and it is stated on every edition. Anything older appears in the executive summary as context, never as an entry.

An empty theme stays empty. Where a theme has no qualifying item it is listed in the coverage table with the count it earned and rendered as no section at all — never as a zero bar and never padded with stale material.

Suppression. A story that ran in either of the previous two editions is not repeated unless the day adds a fact, and the new fact must be what the entry is about.
