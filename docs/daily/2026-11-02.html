A stalled regulator pushed Australia's biggest outback AI data centre off schedule

Energy North has scrapped the target start date for its Project Ares data centre in the Northern Territory, Reuters reported on 2 November. The developer had given a "first power" date of 28 June 2028 in regulatory filings four months ago. The Territory's environment regulator said on 7 October that it was suspending its seven-week deadline to decide what hurdles the project must clear. The 1-gigawatt facility would sit on a cattle ranch 683 kilometres from the nearest city, alongside a gas turbine, a solar farm and an airstrip. The regulator warned of ten impacts, from the strain on water systems to the disruption of Aboriginal sacred sites.

It is the largest Australian AI data centre to slip so far, which shows the binding constraint is permitting rather than capital. The regulator paused because the federal government is still drafting policy expectations for data centres and AI infrastructure, so the project is waiting on a national framework that does not yet exist. For an AUNZ developer, timetable risk now sits in the regulator's queue, not the boardroom.

Frontier models & capability claims 1 story

2

DeepSeek open-sourced a coding agent built to watch a team's chat

DeepSeek released Super-Agent, an experimental coding agent, on 1 November. The Chinese lab published the code under the name dsh-superagent with an MIT licence. The agent works on one GitHub repository and can also listen to Feishu, a workplace chat app. When an approved person asks it for work, it opens its own session and replies in the original thread, for example with a pull request. DeepSeek calls it a research prototype and "not an official DeepSeek product". Bloomberg reported the release is aimed at Anthropic's Claude Code.

It is the open-weight play applied to agents: DeepSeek is commoditising the coding assistant that US labs sell. The stated design goal, to take as little human attention as possible, is also the risk, because an agent acting for a whole team can spend that attention rather than save it. An AUNZ enterprise now has a free, inspectable coding agent, with no support and a caution to review the code first.

Market & geopolitics 1 story

3

Apple confirmed Apple Intelligence in China, powered by Alibaba's Qwen

Apple confirmed on 31 October that Apple Intelligence will launch in China, with Alibaba's Qwen model built in. The first features arrive in a developer beta from 4 November, then publicly in December with iOS 27.3, Apple said in a post on its Chinese newsroom. Qwen will run in Siri, Writing Tools and Visual Intelligence. The rollout includes Live Translation, Genmoji and notification summaries. Apple said "Siri AI" and its other next-generation features are not part of the first release. Chinese approval had kept Apple Intelligence out of the mainland since 2024.

It is the template for how a US model vendor enters a market that will not take an American model directly: pair with a local one and ship what is approved. For AUNZ users it is mostly a signal, but it reframes the sovereignty debate, where a local model is now the price of entry rather than a preference. Australia's own AI procurement will be asked the same question.

Regulation & obligation 1 story

4

New York opened registration for frontier AI developers under the RAISE Act

New York began registering large frontier AI developers with the state from 1 November, under the Responsible AI Safety and Education (RAISE) Act. Governor Kathy Hochul announced the step on 21 September. Developers above the law's revenue and training-compute thresholds must register with the new Office of Digital Innovation, Governance, Integrity and Trust, or DIGIT, inside the Department of Financial Services. From January 2027 they must meet the safety standards and report critical incidents within 72 hours. The governor said she is exploring further steps.

It is the first US state registration regime for frontier developers, and it lands while federal action stalls. The duty is thin beside the EU's, but it is enforceable, which makes it the reference point other states will copy. For an AUNZ board it is a reminder that the operating rule for a global model is now set by the strictest jurisdiction it serves.

Key to this page how to read it

Impact — the only coloured axis

How far the risk, obligation or capability position moves. Impact is not harm: half of what this digest carries — a model release, a research result, a funding round — is not a harm at all. The band is driven by precedent, blast radius, reversibility, structural change and sector criticality, not by a severity dictionary borrowed from cyber incidents.

Obligation — does it bind an AUNZ organisation?

Rendered only when the band is not None; a pill reading “Obligation: None” on every card is noise. Proposed means text exists; Signalled means a minister or a government has said it will — a two-stage ministerial statement stays Signalled. Where an obligation is claimed, the card names the instrument and its operative provision.

Evidence — how established is the claim?

Orthogonal to verification, and the reason this product exists separately from the cyber digest: an AI capability claim usually cannot be verified at all. A vendor testifying about its own model is Verified (they did say it) and simultaneously a Vendor claim (the capability is unreplicated). Both badges render.

Three rules that shape what appears

Freshness is a state-change test, not a date test. An item qualifies when something moved — a model shipped, a rule commenced, a vulnerability was exploited, a subpoena was served, a paper published. The window is 48 hours, 72 across a weekend bridge, and it is stated on every edition. Anything older appears in the executive summary as context, never as an entry.

An empty theme stays empty. Where a theme has no qualifying item it is listed in the coverage table with the count it earned and rendered as no section at all — never as a zero bar and never padded with stale material.

Suppression. A story that ran in either of the previous two editions is not repeated unless the day adds a fact, and the new fact must be what the entry is about.
