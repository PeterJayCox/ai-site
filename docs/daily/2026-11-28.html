The Bank of England has started pricing AI build-out as a stability risk

The Bank of England published its November Financial Stability Report on 26 November, after the Financial Policy Committee met on 19 November. The committee said risks to the global financial system had intensified as conflict in the Middle East pushed up inflation and interest rates. Hyperscalers building AI data centres were on course to issue more than US$305 billion of debt this year. The committee said the sector's broader debt issuance exceeded the annual borrowing of most governments, including Britain's. It called the financing increasingly complex, circular and opaque, which makes stress in one area more likely to reach another. It said frontier AI capabilities had grown since July, notably the sophistication of AI agents and open-weight models. The committee cited recent incidents as evidence that individual agents and multi-agent systems are hard to keep aligned.

Why it matters

This is a system-level regulator treating AI build-out as a balance-sheet question rather than a technology one. The operative word is circular. When vendors finance customers who buy their capacity, a fall in sentiment hits both sides of the same ledger at once. The agentic-AI passage carries the second risk, which is operational. A failure at a shared model or agent supplier is a single point of failure across many regulated firms, which is why cyber resilience now sits inside financial stability. Nothing in the report binds anyone. Supervision follows through the PRA.

Taiwan's economy is now a single bet on AI capex, and the forecast says so

Taiwan's statistics agency raised its 2026 growth forecast to 13.28 per cent on 27 November, from 11.05 per cent in August. That would be the island's fastest expansion since 1978. Third-quarter GDP grew 14.39 per cent year on year, and the first two quarters were revised up to 15.44 and 13.31 per cent. Real exports of goods and services rose 21.65 per cent in the quarter as AI hardware demand held. Real gross capital formation grew 26.74 per cent, led by machinery and equipment. The agency put 2027 growth at 6.51 per cent, less than half this year's pace.

Why it matters

The 2027 number is the informative one. It still assumes AI infrastructure demand outruns supply, so it describes a slowing from a very high base rather than a plateau. That matters upstream of every Australian and New Zealand build: memory, substrates and advanced packaging stay tight while Taiwanese suppliers add capacity against multi-year orders. A forecast that doubles in a quarter also tells you how little visibility the agency has. Chip output is booked years ahead, and the rest of the economy is riding it.

3

A video model ran at vendor-API speed on non-Nvidia silicon

Hugging Face engineers ran Lightricks' LTX-2.5 video model on an AWS Trainium3 instance and produced a 10-second clip with sound in 22 to 23 seconds, on half the machine. They had a trn3.48xlarge with 64 NeuronCores for a month under an AWS partnership, and spent the final five days on video. Their comparison was LTX's own hosted API, which the company says renders a 10-second 1080p clip in 23.7 seconds. The authors set CUDA aside, because the only published Nvidia figure was 6.8 seconds on four B200 GPUs with no detail on how it was measured. Shots were chained from a JSON script of prompts, seeds and frame handoffs.

Why it matters

The interesting claim is parity, not speed. Video generation has been the workload most tightly bound to one accelerator ecosystem, and a diffusers pipeline matching a hosted API on other silicon is a data point against that lock-in. Treat it as a floor. It is one model, one machine, no optimisation budget, and a test run by parties with an interest in the hardware performing well. The write-up is candid about its own limits, which is why the number is worth reading at all.

A Blackwell rack turned up in Shanghai in a loan filing

A GB300 NVL72 rack is installed at a Shanghai data centre called the Yangtze River Delta Project, according to documents filed with Chinese regulators in November and reviewed by Bloomberg. Shanghai Shuoyao listed the rack by serial number and pledged it as collateral for a 96 million yuan loan from Shanghai Innovation Bank. Bloomberg matched the serial number through a public warranty database to a rack built by Taiwan's Gigabyte, which declined to say who first bought it. A second firm, Shanghai Kezhan, described a GB200 server at the site in papers for a 90 million yuan loan and disclosed 32 H200 servers. A third, Shanghai Longjie, pledged Hopper-generation servers from Super Micro and Dell against a 200 million yuan loan. The site is designed to hold up to 800,000 servers. Megaspeed, added to the US Entity List this month, denies wrongdoing, and Bloomberg found no evidence that it diverted the hardware. Nvidia did not respond.

Why it matters

Export control has been enforced at the shipping document. This shows the trail is easier to read a year later, in a bank filing. Each of these placements was disclosed to a Chinese lender or regulator. The hardware's presence was legible to someone long before it was legible in Washington. That is the case for the rule that took effect on 19 November. It covers computing capacity wherever it sits, not the box that crosses a border. For Australian and New Zealand buyers the effect is price and lead time, not supply. The same racks are what regional campuses are queued for.

5

SAP is cutting into its own workforce to pay for AI

SAP is preparing to cut a sizeable number of jobs, two people familiar with the plans told Reuters on 27 November. Bloomberg reported the night before that the company was weighing at least 10,000 cuts in an AI-driven restructuring. SAP employs about 113,000 people and its works council has not been informed. Chief executive Christian Klein and finance chief Dominik Asam had signalled at investor events that the workforce would shrink. The company reviewed 8,000 roles in early 2024, then offset those cuts with hiring elsewhere. In October an SAP executive said it was replacing some expensive AI models with cheaper alternatives, targeting more than US$2 billion in gains.

Why it matters

SAP is the test case for whether AI restructuring lands on vendors or on their customers. Its software runs payroll and accounting at most large firms, so a cut this size is a claim that the maintenance work behind enterprise systems is now automatable. Watch the sequencing. The company is cheapening the models it runs while cutting the people who integrate them. Australian and New Zealand SAP customers should expect the same efficiency argument at renewal, with fewer account staff to test it against.

6

OpenAI's fight with Apple is about the device, not the model

OpenAI sued Apple in a California court on 27 November, the Financial Times reported. The claim accuses Apple of stealing trade secrets and of blocking OpenAI's access to hardware suppliers. Neither company responded to Reuters' request for comment. Apple sued OpenAI and two former employees in July, alleging they carried confidential information into OpenAI's consumer hardware effort. OpenAI denied those allegations.

Why it matters

Both companies want to own the surface where a consumer meets an assistant, and the supplier-access claim is the commercially important part. If a platform owner can close a rival's hardware pipeline, the competition moves from model quality to distribution. Nothing here changes a product roadmap yet, and a case like this takes years.

Three rules that shape what appears

Freshness is a state-change test, not a date test. An item qualifies when something moved — a model shipped, a rule commenced, a vulnerability was exploited, a subpoena was served, a paper published. The window is 48 hours, 72 across a weekend bridge, and it is stated on every edition. Anything older appears in the executive summary as context, never as an entry.

An empty theme stays empty. Where a theme has no qualifying item it is listed in the coverage table with the count it earned and rendered as no section at all — never as a zero bar and never padded with stale material.

Suppression. A story that ran in either of the previous two editions is not repeated unless the day adds a fact, and the new fact must be what the entry is about.
