The FTC demanded Anthropic executives testify in its consumer-harm probe, hours after the company filed for its listing

The US Federal Trade Commission sent demands on Monday 26 October for Anthropic executives to testify in its consumer-harm investigation, an agency spokesperson told Axios, which first reported the escalation. The FTC is examining both Anthropic and OpenAI and has issued civil investigative demands — span of about 50 pages for each company — seeking documents on security incidents, safeguarding practices, marketing materials and privacy policies; OpenAI was subpoenaed earlier in October, and the agency says investigative demands will go to more AI organisations, with individuals at other companies also receiving demands to testify. The agency's authority is to examine conduct that can be treated as unfair or deceptive to consumers, including how services are charged for and whether users have been misled. Anthropic's filing warned that the investigation could produce significant costs and require model design changes, and that it expects to remain the subject of disproportionate government scrutiny. Why it matters: this is the first time a US federal regulator has moved to put frontier-model executives personally on the record about how their systems behave — written interrogatories can be answered by counsel, testimony cannot — and it establishes the investigatory template an AUNZ regulator would follow, given the Australian standards' promise of mandatory incident reporting. The Australian analogue is still at the standards-drafting stage: the OAIC has published nothing AI-specific since its 23 October annual report and eSafety nothing since 2 October. The finding rests on the agency's own confirmation through one outlet, and no subpoena or filing has been published.

What it is

A sibling to the Cyber Digest, not a section of it. Where the cyber daily slices the day by industry sector, this one slices it by layer — because AI news has no natural industry axis. Every item is rated on independent axes: how far the risk or obligation position moves, whether it binds an AUNZ organisation, how much the capability itself moved, and how well established the claim actually is.

  • Frontier models & capability claimsModels, agents, releases and the claims attached to them
  • Regulation & obligationInstruments, regulators, enforcement, liability, parliamentary process
  • Research & evidencePapers, evaluations, replication, and the integrity of the research pipeline
  • AI security & agentic riskAgent conduct, exploitation in the wild, AI-component vulnerabilities
  • Infrastructure & computeData centres, chips, energy and water, sovereign capacity
  • Market & geopoliticsState competition, policy divergence, funding, monetisation, adoption

How to read it

Each card ends in a single-line readout: source, then one pill per axis, the source tier, the verification gate and the date. Colour on it means one thing — impact severity — and every other axis is monochrome, so a hue never has to mean two things at once.
