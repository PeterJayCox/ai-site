What it will be

A sibling to the Cyber Digest, not a section of it. Where the cyber daily slices the day by industry sector, this one slices it by layer — because AI news has no natural industry axis. Every item is rated on three independent axes: how much the risk or obligation position moves, whether it binds an AUNZ organisation, and how well established the claim actually is.

  • 🧠 Frontier capabilityModels, agents and the claims attached to them
  • 🔬 Research & evidencePapers, evaluations, replication, and the integrity of the research pipeline
  • ⚖️ Regulation & obligationInstruments, regulators, enforcement, liability
  • 🛡️ AI security & agentic riskAgent conduct, exploitation, AI-component vulnerabilities
  • 🏗️ Infrastructure & computeData centres, chips, energy and water
  • 🌐 Market, geopolitics & adoptionState competition, policy divergence, funding, deployment

Why the claim-type badge matters

A cyber advisory can be independently verified — a register entry, a patch, an exploitation observation. A frontier capability claim usually cannot be verified at all: the strongest available statement is that the vendor said it, or that someone with no stake measured it. So every item carries a second badge recording what kind of claim it is — vendor claim, independently evaluated, unreplicated preprint, corroborated or rumoured — and no vendor benchmark is ever presented as an established fact.

That is also why there is no equivalent of a CISA advisory here. Nothing in AI carries a statutory duty to publish, so a non-disclosure is caught by no anchor at all. The digest is built knowing that.
