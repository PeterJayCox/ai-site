Pocock proposed a 5 per cent levy on hyperscale AI data centres, costed at just under $500 million

Independent ACT senator David Pocock proposed a 5 per cent levy on the revenue of data centres "owned or operated by or host[ing] rapidly scaling tech companies", applying to facilities drawing more than 5 megawatts from the grid and excluding co-location centres that rent capacity to non-hyperscale customers. Parliamentary Budget Office modelling released with the proposal puts the take at just under $500 million over four years, scaling toward $1 billion over the coming decade (about $3 billion net by 2036–37), against a forecast $200 billion data-centre build-out; the PBO flagged "a high degree of uncertainty" given "limited information on Australian data centre revenues, customer composition and future market development". Pocock's framing starts the levy on 1 January 2027; the Australian Academy of Science, which has advocated a lower 2.5 per cent levy, welcomed the proposal. Why it matters: it is the first PBO-costed attempt to capture a public share of Australia's AI infrastructure boom, and it lands in the same fortnight as NextDC's guidance collapse and Victoria's data-centre buffer-zone rules — the political economy of data centres is moving from siting to revenue. The obligation is signalled, not commenced: it is a private senator's proposal, not a bill, and the PBO's own caveats are the most load-bearing text in the document.

What it is

A sibling to the Cyber Digest, not a section of it. Where the cyber daily slices the day by industry sector, this one slices it by layer — because AI news has no natural industry axis. Every item is rated on independent axes: how far the risk or obligation position moves, whether it binds an AUNZ organisation, how much the capability itself moved, and how well established the claim actually is.

  • Frontier models & capability claimsModels, agents, releases and the claims attached to them
  • Regulation & obligationInstruments, regulators, enforcement, liability, parliamentary process
  • Research & evidencePapers, evaluations, replication, and the integrity of the research pipeline
  • AI security & agentic riskAgent conduct, exploitation in the wild, AI-component vulnerabilities
  • Infrastructure & computeData centres, chips, energy and water, sovereign capacity
  • Market & geopoliticsState competition, policy divergence, funding, monetisation, adoption

How to read it

Each card ends in a single-line readout: source, then one pill per axis, the source tier, the verification gate and the date. Colour on it means one thing — impact severity — and every other axis is monochrome, so a hue never has to mean two things at once.
